# Product improvement opportunities

## Purpose

The current PhilGEPS Parquet Viewer is no longer only a raw-file browser. It
already combines a search-first researcher experience, process pages, party
pages, release history, data-quality review, and source-row traceability over
18 normalized datasets. Its foundation covers approximately 11.25 million
contracting processes, including records that never reached an award or
contract.

Population figures in this document are regenerated by
`scripts/doc_stats.py`; see "Known review findings" for the as-of snapshot.

This creates an opportunity to combine the strongest ideas from the two
predecessor projects:

- [philgeps-awards-dashboard](https://github.com/csiiiv/philgeps-awards-dashboard):
  approachable spending analytics, entity exploration, trends, filters, and
  visual summaries.
- [philgeps-ocds-schema-analysis](https://github.com/csiiiv/philgeps-ocds-schema-analysis):
  process-oriented OCDS representation, portable identifiers, releases, and
  interoperable JSON.

The current normalized Parquets should remain the authoritative evidence
layer. Analytics, researcher-friendly summaries, and OCDS publications should
be derived products whose assumptions are explicit and reproducible.

Each opportunity below describes:

- **Current state:** what the latest viewer already provides.
- **Target experience:** what the feature could become.
- **Why it matters:** the user or data problem it solves.
- **Implementation approach:** a practical way to build it from the existing
  datasets and application architecture.

## Product guardrails

All improvements should preserve the viewer's existing principles:

1. Source evidence remains one click away.
2. Missing, ambiguous, and conflicting relationships remain visible.
3. Observed values are distinguishable from normalized, derived, defaulted,
   inferred, and provisional values.
4. Analytics declare their population, grain, denominator, and currency rule.
5. Disposable indexes and aggregates accelerate the viewer but never become
   the source of truth.
6. Party clusters are analytical identities, not assertions of legal identity.
7. An award does not imply that a contract exists without contract evidence.

## Known review findings (September 2026)

Facts discovered while verifying this document against the live dataset.
They constrain the roadmap and are tracked as ETL-side tickets in `TODO.md`:

1. **Uniform severity.** All 14.6 million `data_quality_issues` rows carry
   severity `warning`. Priority 5 (quality dashboard) cannot group by
   severity until the ETL rule catalog defines meaningful severities.
2. **`publication_blocking` is near-total.** About 99.96% of issue rows set
   `publication_blocking = true`, which strongly suggests a defaulted value
   rather than a computed one. This flag gates the OCDS export (§19) and must
   be either recomputed or explicitly documented as informational.
3. **Few procurement methods.** `tenders.procurement_method` has only three
   distinct values; method analytics (§11) are low-cardinality unless the
   source layer exposes more.
4. **No tenderer-count evidence.** `tenders.parquet` has no bidder or
   tenderer-count columns. Competition indicators that need them (§11) have
   essentially zero coverage today; they require new ETL extraction, not just
   new views.
5. **Projection memory ceiling.** The party wide-table build OOMs above a
   roughly 6 GB budget (documented in `backend/app/search.py`). Every new
   projection in this roadmap must state its build memory cost and pass a
   measured query-miss justification (see §22).
6. **Counts drift.** Snapshot-dependent numbers (process count, issue count,
   multi-release count) are regenerated by `scripts/doc_stats.py` instead of
   being hand-maintained.

## Priority 1: Make the complete procurement population understandable

### 1. Canonical procurement outcome classification

**Current state**

The viewer displays the preferred tender status, award and contract counts,
quality flags, item links, and unsuccessful or cancelled events. A researcher
can infer what happened, but there is no single outcome field that consistently
describes the process across search results, process pages, exports, and
analytics.

**Target experience**

Every process receives a clearly labeled outcome such as:

- ongoing;
- closed, outcome unknown;
- closed without award;
- awarded;
- partially awarded;
- cancelled;
- unsuccessful; or
- conflicting evidence.

The process page should show both the normalized tender status and the derived
outcome, including a short explanation of the evidence used.

**Why it matters**

Award-only analysis cannot answer how much procurement activity failed,
stopped, or remained unresolved. A canonical outcome makes the complete
dataset understandable without collapsing important distinctions into
"awarded" and "not awarded."

**Implementation approach**

Create a versioned outcome rule over `tenders`, `awards`, `award_items`,
`contracts`, and `data_quality_issues`. Emit an analytical projection keyed by
`process_id` with `outcome`, `outcome_basis`, `rule_version`, and relevant
counts. Keep it disposable and fingerprinted to the authoritative Parquets.
Add fixture cases for each outcome, especially partial awards and contradictory
statuses.

### 2. Procurement coverage and conversion funnel

**Current state**

The catalog reports dataset row counts, and the Data Health page reports
saved-view counts. The interface does not yet present the complete population
as a lifecycle funnel.

**Target experience**

Provide a coverage page showing how many processes were published, closed,
awarded, contracted, cancelled, unsuccessful, or remain indeterminate. Users
should be able to filter the funnel by year, buyer, region, category, and
procurement method, then open the matching processes.

**Why it matters**

This is the clearest demonstration of what the new dataset adds over the
awards dashboard. It makes non-awarded records visible and establishes honest
denominators for award rates and spending statistics.

**Implementation approach**

Aggregate the outcome projection at process grain. Store counts separately
from monetary totals so incomplete values do not remove a process from the
funnel. Each displayed metric should expose its filter definition and link to
the corresponding search or explorer query.

### 3. Evidence-aware lifecycle model

**Current state**

Process pages already show a subway-style timeline with tender publication,
closing, award, contract, and contract-end events. Split awards can appear as
separate branches. Dates use documented fallbacks when a preferred date is
missing.

**Target experience**

Make lifecycle stages and their evidence strength explicit. A timeline event
should indicate whether its date was observed directly, normalized from a
source field, or substituted from a publication date. Missing stages should be
shown as gaps rather than silently omitted.

**Why it matters**

The older OCDS experiment could create an apparently complete contracting
lifecycle from limited source evidence. Explicit event provenance prevents an
award publication date from being mistaken for a contract signing date.

**Implementation approach**

Extend timeline events with `value_origin`, `date_basis`, `source_count`, and
`confidence`. Render small, consistent evidence labels and a "View evidence"
action. Add stage-level validation rules for impossible ordering, such as an
award preceding publication or a contract preceding its award.

### 4. Partial-award and unawarded-item analysis

**Current state**

The process page already links tender items to awards, identifies unawarded
items, shows award lanes, and avoids treating merely listed suppliers as award
winners.

**Target experience**

Add a portfolio view for partial awards: processes with some awarded items,
their awarded and unawarded quantities or values, the suppliers for each lot,
and the time elapsed since closing. Provide separate states for "not awarded"
and "award link missing."

**Why it matters**

Partial procurement cannot be measured reliably from a flat contract table.
The normalized `award_items` relationship is one of the new model's most
valuable advantages and should support analysis beyond a single process page.

**Implementation approach**

Build a process-item fact projection from `tender_items`, `award_items`,
`awards`, and `award_suppliers`. Preserve quantity/unit grain and avoid
allocating award value to items unless the source provides item values. Add
facets and saved views for partially awarded, fully unawarded, and ambiguous
item linkage.

## Priority 2: Turn search into a complete research workspace

### 5. Broader faceted search

**Current state**

The search landing page searches procurement titles and party names. It
supports status and closing-year filters, paged results, party-name versus
resolved-identity scope, and shareable process links.

**Target experience**

Add filters for procuring entity, supplier, procurement method, category,
location, outcome, value range, source period, award presence, contract
presence, and quality status. Active filters should appear as removable chips,
and result counts should update without losing the search text.

**Why it matters**

The complete data becomes useful when researchers can define a population
without learning table names or DuckDB predicates. These dimensions also
recover the best discovery workflows from the awards dashboard.

**Implementation approach**

Extend the disposable search index with low-cardinality facet keys and
precomputed facet dictionaries. Keep high-cardinality buyer and supplier
selection behind typeahead endpoints. Encode all filters in the URL and
translate them into the same canonical query object used by exports.

### 6. Item and category search

**Current state**

Tender items are visible on process pages and in the Advanced Explorer, but
the primary search index is centered on process titles and party names.

**Target experience**

Allow searches such as "laptop," "school meals," or an UNSPSC code to return
processes containing matching tender items. Results should show the matching
item text, quantity, unit, category, award state, and supplier when linked.

**Why it matters**

Procurement titles are often generic. Item-level search lets users study what
government bought rather than only the name of the notice or contractor.

**Implementation approach**

Create an item-search projection keyed by `release_id` and `item_id`, with a
link to the preferred process. Index normalized descriptions and codes without
discarding the original description. Return bounded matching snippets and
deduplicate results at process grain by default, with an optional item-grain
view.

### 7. Related and possibly repeated procurements

**Current state**

Users can navigate explicit relationships and repeated releases within a
process. There is no cross-process discovery for similar notices.

**Target experience**

Show "Related procurements" based on shared buyer, normalized title tokens,
solicitation number, item categories, location, value band, and adjacent dates.
Clearly label these as similarity suggestions, not confirmed duplicates.

**Why it matters**

This helps researchers find rebids, recurring purchases, split notices, and
possible duplicate ingestion while preserving the boundary between evidence
and interpretation.

**Implementation approach**

Start with deterministic candidate generation rather than opaque scoring:
same buyer plus title fingerprint, reference-number similarity, or overlapping
item codes. Store match reasons and a versioned score in a disposable
candidate table. Validate against known multi-release and duplicate cases
before surfacing broad fuzzy matching.

### 8. Source-file and period explorer

**Current state**

From a process or record, users can inspect source files, source URLs, row
ranges, and original PhilGEPS rows. The catalog also checks manifest and
source-layer health.

**Target experience**

Provide a first-class source coverage page organized by year and quarter. It
should show file status, expected versus observed coverage, row counts,
derived-process counts, award rates, quality-issue rates, and suspicious gaps
or spikes.

**Why it matters**

Dataset-wide changes can otherwise be mistaken for real procurement trends.
A missing quarter, schema change, or malformed source file must be visible
before users interpret aggregate results.

**Implementation approach**

Aggregate `manifest.json`, source breadcrumbs, releases, and quality issues by
`source_file_id`. Add comparison checks against neighboring periods and expose
the underlying manifest entry and sample affected rows. Keep anomaly labels
descriptive, not accusatory.

## Priority 3: Add analytics without returning to an award-only model

### 9. Buyer portfolio pages

**Current state**

Party pages list procurements under an observed name or provisional identity
and show role counts, status counts, value by year, and contracts by start
year. Buyer-specific performance views are limited.

**Target experience**

Create buyer portfolio pages with total processes, outcome distribution,
procurement methods, categories, suppliers, tender value, awarded value,
typical timelines, data completeness, and year-over-year trends. Every chart
should link to its supporting process population.

**Why it matters**

Agencies should be evaluated on their complete procurement activity, not only
their successful awards. Buyer pages can reveal operational patterns and data
quality differences without implying wrongdoing.

**Implementation approach**

Reuse the existing party identity scope and add role-filtered process facts.
Compute counts at process grain and values at the appropriate tender, award,
or contract grain. Label shared award and contract values carefully to prevent
double counting across suppliers.

### 10. Supplier portfolio and concentration analysis

**Current state**

Supplier pages support name and provisional-identity scopes, procurement
history, role summaries, value trends, contract counts, aliases, and identity
evidence.

**Target experience**

Add wins by buyer, category, geography, method, and year; repeat-buyer share;
award counts; joint-venture participation; and concentration measures. Users
should be able to switch between exact observed name and provisional identity
without changing the meaning silently.

**Why it matters**

The awards dashboard demonstrated the value of contractor-centric research.
The current relationship model can reproduce it more accurately because it
distinguishes party mentions, supplier assertions, awards, and contracts.

**Implementation approach**

Build supplier-award facts at `(release_id, award_id, supplier assertion)`
grain. Use explicit allocation rules: count an award once per award for market
totals, but show participation counts per supplier. Never present a shared
award value as exclusive supplier revenue. Carry `joint_venture`, link status,
and identity scope into every aggregate response.

### 11. Procurement method and competition indicators

**Current state**

Method and status mappings are available in tender data, and unresolved
mappings appear in saved quality views. Competition is not yet summarized as
a research subject.

**Target experience**

Show process counts, award rates, values, duration, and supplier concentration
by procurement method. Where source evidence permits, add tenderer counts,
single-bid indicators, repeat-winner patterns, and method changes over time.

**Why it matters**

Method-level analysis can identify where open competition is common, where
alternative methods dominate, and where the source data is too incomplete to
support a conclusion.

**Implementation approach**

Begin with indicators directly supported by existing fields. Attach a
coverage rate to every metric, such as "tenderer count available for 18% of
matching processes." Treat missing tenderer data as unknown, not zero. Add
competition extensions only if the source observations genuinely contain the
required evidence.

### 12. Timeliness and delay analysis

**Current state**

Individual process timelines show publication, closing, award, and contract
events. The viewer does not aggregate elapsed time across processes.

**Target experience**

Provide distributions for publication-to-close, close-to-award,
award-to-contract, and contract duration. Allow comparison by buyer, method,
category, year, and outcome, while flagging impossible or fallback-derived
dates separately.

**Why it matters**

Timeliness is a useful operational measure that includes successful and
unsuccessful procurement. It can also reveal source-quality problems such as
reversed or implausible dates.

**Implementation approach**

Create duration fields only when both event endpoints meet an explicit
evidence threshold. Publish valid-row coverage, median, percentiles, and
outlier counts rather than only averages. Link each bucket or outlier to the
supporting processes and source evidence.

### 13. Budget, award, and contract value reconciliation

**Current state**

Process pages compare tender value with summed awards and show contract value.
Mixed or missing currencies prevent comparison, and unusual ratios receive a
plain-language visual signal.

**Target experience**

Add dataset-wide analysis of tender-to-award and award-to-contract differences,
including partial awards, multiple awards, missing values, and values above the
tender reference. Show comparable and non-comparable populations separately.

**Why it matters**

Users want to study savings and increases, but naive subtraction produces
misleading results when lots, currencies, duplicate awards, or missing links
are involved.

**Implementation approach**

Build a value-reconciliation fact table with `comparison_status`, numerator,
denominator, currency rule, award count, item coverage, and exclusion reason.
Use the same calculation in process pages, analytics, and exports. Document
that a tender reference value is not necessarily a final budget or a savings
baseline.

### 14. Geographic and category exploration

**Current state**

Location and classification fields are available in normalized records and
the Advanced Explorer, but there is no dedicated geographic or category
research view.

**Target experience**

Offer region, province, city, and procurement-category summaries with process
counts, outcomes, buyers, suppliers, and values. A map can be added only where
location quality is sufficient; a ranked table should remain the primary
precise view.

**Why it matters**

Geographic and category exploration makes the data useful for regional
journalism, sector research, and demand analysis while exposing gaps in source
classification.

**Implementation approach**

Normalize location keys conservatively and retain original strings. Track the
basis of each location: buyer address, delivery area, or source-level region.
Do not mix these meanings in one metric. Add category crosswalks as versioned
reference data and show unmapped categories explicitly.

## Priority 4: Strengthen trust, validation, and collaboration

### 15. Field-level provenance and derivation explanations

**Current state**

Record-level traceability is strong: users can move from derived records to
source rows and compare normalized with cleaned observations. Process pages
also expose selection rules and quality flags. Provenance is not consistently
attached to each summarized field.

**Target experience**

For important fields such as status, buyer, amount, supplier, and event date,
provide a compact explanation of where the value came from and whether it was
observed, normalized, selected, inferred, or defaulted.

**Why it matters**

The more convenient the compiled view becomes, the easier it is for users to
mistake a derivation for a source fact. Field-level explanations preserve
trust without forcing every user into the raw tables.

**Implementation approach**

Define a common metadata shape with `origin`, `rule`, `rule_version`,
`source_fields`, and `source_count`. Add it first to high-risk fields rather
than every column. Render details on demand to keep process pages scannable.

### 16. Relationship-confidence review

**Current state**

The ETL records matched, missing, and ambiguous relationships for contracts,
award suppliers, party aliases, and identity candidates. Some views show link
issues, but incomplete relationships are not yet represented consistently in
every navigation path.

**Target experience**

Use one visual and API vocabulary for confirmed, inferred, ambiguous, missing,
and conflicting links. A user should be able to open the evidence for a link
or see why navigation is unavailable.

**Why it matters**

False links are more damaging than visible gaps. Consistent relationship
states prevent the interface from turning uncertain ETL output into an
apparently verified fact.

**Implementation approach**

Create shared backend and frontend types for relationship status. Require
summary builders to return status and reason alongside linked IDs. Add tests
that missing and ambiguous links remain non-clickable but inspectable. This
directly completes the open TODO to preserve incomplete relationships.

### 17. Actionable data-quality dashboard

**Current state**

The Data Health page displays counts for configured saved views, and the
Advanced Explorer can inspect issue records with rule version and source
links. The present issue dataset is dominated by a single severity.

**Target experience**

Group issues by rule, dataset, source period, buyer, outcome, and publication
impact. Show newly introduced, increasing, decreasing, and resolved issue
populations between ETL snapshots. Each summary should open a bounded review
queue.

**Why it matters**

Thirteen million issue rows are not useful as one undifferentiated backlog.
Prioritization should identify which rules affect publication, which sources
introduced problems, and whether a pipeline change improved the data.

**Implementation approach**

Build a disposable quality-summary projection and define meaningful
severities in the ETL rule catalog. Add snapshot comparison keyed by stable
issue signatures rather than generated row position. Keep saved views as the
configuration layer for drill-through queues.

### 18. Review cases, notes, and reproducible findings

**Current state**

Queries and process links are shareable, but the application is read-only and
does not persist reviewer notes or groups of related records.

**Target experience**

Allow maintainers to create a case containing processes, parties, source rows,
quality issues, notes, and a frozen query definition. A case export should be
a small JSON or Markdown bundle, not a modification of any Parquet file.

**Why it matters**

Complex validation findings often span several records and source files.
Reproducible cases make handoff, discussion, regression testing, and ETL rule
design much easier.

**Implementation approach**

Start with local file-based case exports and imports so the viewer remains
local-only and source data stays immutable. Define stable references and
dataset fingerprints in the case format. Add multi-user persistence only
after authentication and deployment requirements are decided.

## Priority 5: Publish and exchange the data safely

### 19. Validated OCDS export

**Current state**

The normalized tables are OCDS-aligned, but publishing OCDS JSON is explicitly
outside the current MVP. The earlier schema-analysis project generated OCDS
records but sometimes represented inferred lifecycle stages too strongly.

**Target experience**

Provide downloadable OCDS release and record packages for a process or a
filtered collection. Exports should pass schema validation and carry enough
companion metadata to explain PhilGEPS-specific mappings and inferences.

**Why it matters**

OCDS makes the data interoperable with external research tools without
forcing the internal evidence model to lose source detail or conform to one
compiled document.

**Implementation approach**

Create a separate, versioned exporter from the normalized Parquets. Map only
supported fields, omit unsupported lifecycle objects, and never synthesize a
contract from an award alone. Validate packages automatically and publish a
mapping document, coverage report, extension list, and rejected-record report.

### 20. Research API and reproducible exports

**Current state**

The application exposes bounded table queries, saved views, search, process,
party, timeline, provenance, and export endpoints. It is designed for local
validation and limits exports to protect memory and response size.

**Target experience**

Offer documented analytical endpoints for common questions: process outcomes,
buyer portfolios, supplier participation, methods, categories, time periods,
and quality coverage. Every export should include the query, dataset version,
row grain, truncation status, and calculation notes.

**Why it matters**

Researchers should not need to reconstruct complex joins, and a CSV without
its population definition is difficult to reproduce or audit.

**Implementation approach**

Keep generic table access for maintainers, then add purpose-built aggregate
endpoints over reviewed fact projections. Use cursor pagination or background
jobs for large exports. Package a metadata sidecar with every result and keep
spreadsheet-formula protection already used by the viewer.

### 21. Versioned public data snapshots

**Current state**

Source manifests and cache fingerprints exist, and rule versions appear in
several derived outputs. There is no single public snapshot identity tying
together every Parquet, rule version, source file, and export.

**Target experience**

Assign each complete build a snapshot identifier with generation time, source
inventory, row counts, file checksums, schema versions, ETL rule versions, and
known limitations. Allow users to compare snapshots and cite one permanently.

**Why it matters**

Analytics change when source files or normalization rules change. Versioned
snapshots make results reproducible and distinguish corrected data from
historical output.

**Implementation approach**

Generate a build manifest after validation succeeds. Reference it from the
catalog, API responses, exports, case files, and OCDS packages. Add schema and
row-count checks that prevent incomplete builds from being labeled publishable.

## Priority 6: Prepare the application for scale and wider use

### 22. Performance targets and benchmark harness

**Current state**

The viewer uses bounded DuckDB work, signed keyset pagination, memoization,
and disposable provenance and search indexes. Several cold queries still take
seconds, and the TODO calls for systematic benchmarks and latency targets.

**Target experience**

Define measurable targets for search, process pages, party pages, provenance,
facets, aggregates, and exports under cold and warm conditions. Track memory,
spill size, cache build time, and degraded fallback behavior.

**Why it matters**

New analytics can easily turn full-table joins into an unstable application.
Performance budgets help decide when a projection is justified and prevent
convenience caches from growing without control.

**Implementation approach**

Add a repeatable benchmark script with representative IDs, common search
terms, high-cardinality parties, and worst-case filters. Record results by
snapshot and machine profile. Build new projections only after a measured
query misses its target, and fingerprint every cache to data plus rule version.

### 23. Public deployment mode

**Current state**

The viewer binds to `127.0.0.1` and is intentionally a local validation
workbench. Authentication, rate limiting, persistent review state, and public
service guarantees are deferred.

**Target experience**

Support a separately configured public researcher mode with bounded APIs,
download policies, observability, accessible responsive layouts, and no
maintainer-only raw capabilities exposed by default.

**Why it matters**

The complete dataset is most valuable when journalists, researchers, public
officials, and citizens can explore it, but a local audit tool should not be
placed on the internet unchanged.

**Implementation approach**

Keep local and public modes distinct. Add an explicit deployment profile,
reverse-proxy rules, rate limits, request-size limits, cache warming, health
metrics, privacy and security review, and a public endpoint allowlist. Retain
the local viewer for unrestricted source inspection and ETL validation.

### 24. Plain-language methodology and metric disclosures

**Current state**

The About page explains the data model, identifiers, preferred releases, and
catalog statistics. Individual views include several honest-data notes and
plain-language quality explanations.

**Target experience**

Give every aggregate metric a short, consistent methodology disclosure:
population, grain, numerator, denominator, exclusions, currency handling,
identity scope, and last data snapshot. Provide deeper documentation through
links rather than filling the working interface with instructions.

**Why it matters**

The complete dataset supports more powerful conclusions, which increases the
cost of an unclear denominator or hidden inference. Metric disclosures allow
users to interpret charts correctly and reproduce them independently.

**Implementation approach**

Define metrics in a versioned registry shared by backend calculations,
frontend labels, API documentation, and exports. Treat metric definitions like
code: review them, test them against fixtures, and record changes in the build
manifest.

## Suggested delivery roadmap

### Phase 1: Establish shared analytical foundations

0. **Adopt one shared derivation-metadata shape.** A single record shape
   (`origin`, `rule`, `rule_version`, `source_fields`, `basis`,
   `confidence`, `source_count`) that the outcome rules (§1), field-level
   provenance (§15), relationship-confidence states (§16), and the metric
   registry (§24) all extend, instead of each defining its own vocabulary.
   *(Done 2026-09-16: `backend/app/derivation.py`; outcome funnel and
   relationship badges both carry it.)*
1. Add benchmark targets and baseline measurements first — before designing
   any projection, because the baseline decides whether the outcome
   projection fits the build memory ceiling (see Known review finding 5).
   *(Done 2026-09-16: `scripts/benchmark.py`, docs/12.)*
2. Define canonical process outcomes and their tests. *(Done 2026-09-16:
   docs/13, Coverage tab, outcome facets/filters, process badge.)*
3. Standardize relationship status and derivation metadata using the shared
   shape from step 0. *(Done 2026-09-16: docs/14, `relationship-status-v1`;
   award-supplier entries expose normalized `link` states plus derivation
   metadata; UI renders `linked/missing/ambiguous` explicitly.)*
4. Create the process-level analytical fact projection. *(Partially done:
   the `outcomes` table in `search_index.duckdb` covers the lifecycle
   dimension; the fuller fact table remains open.)*
5. Generate a versioned build manifest. *(Done 2026-09-16: stored in the
   index `meta` table, served at `GET /api/index/manifest`; see docs/13.)*

This phase prevents later dashboards from implementing conflicting
definitions. Effort: M–L overall; step 1 (benchmarks) is S and unblocks
everything else.

### Phase 2: Demonstrate complete-data value

1. Launch the procurement coverage and conversion funnel (hard-depends on
   Phase 1 outcomes).
2. Expand search facets and add outcome filters.
3. Add partial-award and unawarded-item views.
4. Add source-period coverage and data-quality summaries (severity grouping
   is blocked on the ETL severity ticket; see Known review finding 1).

This phase most clearly differentiates the current viewer from the former
awards-only experience. Effort: L.

### Phase 3: Restore and improve portfolio analytics

1. Build buyer portfolio pages (depends on outcomes for outcome
   distributions and on the §16 vocabulary for link states).
2. Expand supplier portfolio and concentration views.
3. Add method, timeliness, value-reconciliation, category, and geography
   analysis.
4. Ensure every aggregate links back to its supporting processes.

This phase recovers the strongest analytical ideas from the awards dashboard
without excluding unsuccessful procurement. Effort: L.

### Phase 4: Interoperability and publication

1. Build and validate the OCDS exporter (blocked until the
   `publication_blocking` default is recomputed or documented; see Known
   review finding 2).
2. Add purpose-built research APIs and reproducible export metadata.
3. Test snapshot publication and citation workflows.
4. Design a hardened public deployment mode separately from the local
   validation workbench.

Effort: L, plus the ETL-side tickets above.

## Recommended first implementation slice

The best first slice is a versioned `process_outcomes` projection plus a
coverage page and search filter, built **on top of the shared
derivation-metadata shape and a measured benchmark baseline** (both change
the design of the slice itself, so they come first). It has a contained
implementation surface and immediately communicates the central advantage
of the current data model: PhilGEPS procurement can be studied as a complete
population, not only as a collection of awarded contracts.

The slice should include:

1. A documented outcome taxonomy and decision order.
2. Fixture coverage for every outcome and conflict case.
3. Outcome counts by year and procurement method.
4. A search facet and process-page outcome badge.
5. Drill-through from every count to the exact supporting processes.
6. Source and rule metadata explaining each classification, expressed in
   the shared derivation-metadata shape.

Once this definition is trusted, most later analytics can build on it without
reinterpreting the lifecycle independently.
